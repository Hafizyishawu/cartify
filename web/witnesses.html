Why anyone else has to sign

A Merkle tree stops us editing history. It does not stop us keeping two histories. Whoever holds the log's signing key can sign one tree head for you and a different one for someone else, both internally consistent, both serving valid proofs. Nothing in the mathematics detects that.

A witness holds one piece of state: the last tree head it signed. When we offer a new one, it checks that the new head genuinely continues the same log, with nothing removed or inserted, and only then cosigns. If we ever kept a second history, the new head would not line up with what the witness is holding, and it would refuse.

Witnesses exist to protect you from us. That is the point, and it is why the count above matters more than anything else on this site.

What we ask of a witness

Very little, deliberately. A witness never stores our log, never sees a registered file, never evaluates a claim, and never handles anyone's personal data. It verifies one property, that the log has only ever grown and there is only one of it, and signs. Seconds of computer time per interval.

We do not pay witnesses. A witness we funded would be us wearing a hat, and the independence is the entire product.

Anchors are not witnesses

We also publish each tree head to places we do not control: a timestamping authority and a public timestamp chain. Those bound how far back a record can be dated, which is useful, but a timestamping authority will stamp two conflicting tree heads without noticing. Anchors never count toward the quorum, and there is no code path that would let them.

Interested in witnessing?

We are looking for organisations with a reason to care about durable provenance and no reason to favour us: archives, libraries, university departments, photographers' and journalists' bodies, and other provenance services willing to watch each other. The software is open and the operational burden is a scheduled job.
