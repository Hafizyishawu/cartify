How it works

Certifiles records that a file existed, in a particular form, claimed by a particular identity, at a point in time. Everything below is about making that one sentence checkable by someone who does not trust us.

What happens when a work is registered

  1. Your browser computes the file's SHA-256 fingerprint and a few perceptual fingerprints. The file itself is never uploaded. We hold no copy and want none.
  2. Those fingerprints, your identity, the strength of that identity, and what you are claiming go into a record.
  3. The record is appended to a public log at a permanent position. Positions are never reassigned, and nothing is ever edited or deleted.
  4. The log's tree head is signed, published where we cannot rewrite it, and cosigned by independent witnesses.

What anyone can then check, without us

The whole log is published as plain files. Given a file, a browser or the command line verifier can prove that a record for it sits at a specific position in a log whose head independent parties have signed. No account, no API, no working Certifiles server.

That is deliberate. A provenance service whose verification depends on the provider being alive, solvent and honest has not provided provenance.

Two things this cannot do

It cannot tell you a file is not AI generated

A generated image produces a fingerprint exactly like a photograph does. Registration proves a file existed and was claimed, not how it was made. What Certifiles offers is the opposite direction: an artist can declare how a work was made, and that declaration is recorded permanently alongside it. Absence of a record proves nothing at all, because most work in the world is registered nowhere.

It cannot prove who created something

A fingerprint proves a file is unchanged. It says nothing about authorship. What the log records is who registered first, which is how copyright registration, patent filing and trademark have always worked. That puts the burden on authors to register before publishing, and we would rather say so plainly than imply a guarantee we cannot keep.

Backdating, and why anchors exist

The attack that matters is not forging a record. It is moving one earlier in time, since disputes turn on who was first. We publish each tree head to a timestamping authority and a public timestamp chain, both outside our control, so a record cannot later be claimed to predate an anchor that already covered it.

Forks, and why witnesses exist

A tamper evident log stops us editing history and does not stop us keeping two histories, one for you and one for someone else. Only independent parties checking that the log has a single continuous history close that, which is why the witness page is the most important page here and why the status page shows the current count.

Near match monitoring

Perceptual fingerprints let us surface later registrations that resemble your work. This catches ordinary copying. It is not proof: two unrelated images can score close by coincidence, and someone who knows how the measure works can evade it. Alerts show the raw distances and never a verdict, because a false alert is an accusation handed to you about someone who may have done nothing.
