For artists

Register a work before you publish it, get a permanent public record you can point anyone at, and be told when something resembling it is registered later.

What you get

  • A permanent record at a fixed position in a public log, with a link you can put in a portfolio, a bio, a listing or a dispute.
  • A proof anyone can check without an account and without trusting us, including if this company no longer exists.
  • Monitoring, which tells you when work resembling yours is registered by someone else.
  • Your files stay yours. We never receive them. Fingerprints are computed in your browser and only those are sent.

Register before you publish

The log records who registered first. If someone else registers your work before you do, the log will say so, because the log reports what happened rather than what should have. This is the same rule as copyright registration and patent filing, and it means the habit that protects you is registering at the point of finishing, not at the point of trouble.

If you have a back catalogue already published, register it now. A prior art claim process exists for work that predates your account, and it is weaker than registering first, which is the honest position.

What we will not claim

Certifiles cannot tell you whether something was made by a person or a machine, and any service that says it can is overselling. What it can do is let you state how your work was made and record that statement permanently, so the claim is yours and dated rather than argued about later.

It also cannot prove authorship. It proves registration. That distinction is the whole design, and it is why the record page tells you what a record does not establish as clearly as what it does.
