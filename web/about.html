How it works

About Certifiles

A public record of authorship claims, built so that believing it does not require trusting the people who run it.

The problem we are actually solving

Creators have no cheap way to establish that a work existed in a particular form at a particular time. Copyright exists from creation, but proving when is the part that fails in practice, usually at the exact moment it matters.

Certifiles is a public, append only log of authorship claims with proofs anyone can check. It grew out of Cartify, a certification tool for artists, and the log is the part of that idea that generalises to any file.

Why you should not have to trust us

We hold the log's signing key. That makes us the most capable attacker on our own system: we could try to backdate a record, or serve one history to you and another to someone else. A design that asks you to believe we would not is not a design.

So the guarantees are structural rather than promised:

  • A record is valid only if it is in the log. A signature alone proves nothing, so a stolen key produces records that fail verification.
  • Independent witnesses cosign every tree head, so we cannot keep two histories without one of them noticing and refusing.
  • Tree heads are published where we cannot rewrite them, which bounds how far back anything can be dated.
  • The whole log is published as plain files, so verification works with no Certifiles server in the path, including if this company ends.
  • We never receive your files. Only fingerprints, computed in your browser.

What we hold, and what we cannot delete

The log contains fingerprints, an opaque identity identifier, and what was claimed. It contains no names, no email addresses and no files. That is not only for privacy: an append only log cannot honour a deletion request, so anything that might need deleting must never enter it. Account details live in an ordinary database where erasure works normally.

A record itself cannot be removed. It can be superseded or marked disputed. We would rather you know that before registering than discover it afterwards.

Where this is today

The log, the proofs, the publication format and the verifiers are built and tested. No witness has yet agreed to cosign, which means the log currently runs at stage 0 and every record says so. The status page shows the live position rather than a claim about it.

Recruiting witnesses is the work that no amount of engineering completes, and it is the reason the paid tier is closed. If your organisation might witness, the witness page describes what it involves.

Open source

The verifier is open source, and it has to be. "You do not have to trust me" is only true if you can check the claim with software we did not hand you at the moment of checking.
